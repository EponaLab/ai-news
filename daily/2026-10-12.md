### **3. Security Teams Rush to Publish Playbooks as Autonomous Agents Power Real Attacks**

**Three major security developments:**

**Kaspersky Autonomous Agent Security Framework:**
Released comprehensive report on securing autonomous AI agents in enterprise infrastructures, framing language models as **untrusted components** and applying Cyber Immunity design principles:
- Strict isolation
- Minimal privileges
- Defensive defaults
- Agent-specific architecture patterns

**Gambit Security Automated Hacking Report:**
Detailed fully automated hacking services using open-source AI tools to continuously attack hundreds of sites. Highlights how autonomous agents industrialize credential theft and data breaches at relatively low per-attack costs.

**RSA Agent ID Platform:**
Industry roundup flagged new governance tool aimed at discovering, protecting, and governing AI agents across heavily regulated organizations—addressing the shadow agent problem.

**Critical message:** Security and compliance leaders can no longer treat AI agents as experimental side projects. They are now both powerful internal tools and live components in attack chains. Organizations must:
- Inventory every agent with system or network access
- Apply isolation and privilege minimization (Kaspersky-style)
- Centralize agent identity and policy management
- Track ownership, access scope, and incident handling procedures

### **The Shift from Meetings to Workflows**

Cisco's Webex agentic AI framework signals an important architectural shift: collaboration platforms evolving from passive communication tools to active workflow orchestration hubs. This represents the logical next step after "AI assistants in meetings"—agents that own entire coordination workflows before, during, and after human interaction.

**Key insight:** The winners in enterprise collaboration won't be determined by who has the best meeting transcription or summarization. They'll be determined by who can orchestrate autonomous workflows across the entire collaboration lifecycle while maintaining proper governance.

**Strategic question for builders:** Is your agent a feature inside a tool, or infrastructure for workflow orchestration? The latter commands higher enterprise value and creates stickier moats.

### **Open Source Agent Infrastructure Comes of Age**

Nous Research's $90M raise at $1.5B valuation with 24M+ clones and 2.5% of global token usage represents a milestone: **open-source agents achieving production-grade distribution and enterprise market validation simultaneously**.

**What changed:** Open-source AI agents historically lived in research repos and hobbyist projects. Hermes crossed into genuine enterprise deployment (hence "Hermes for Businesses") while maintaining open-source roots.

**Strategic implication:** The agent stack is bifurcating:
- **Closed platforms** (OpenAI Dots, Google Gemini agents): Easier deployment, vendor lock-in, compliance dependencies
- **Open infrastructure** (Hermes, self-hosted alternatives): Customization, data sovereignty, integration complexity

Neither dominates universally. Enterprise buyers need both: closed platforms for speed-to-value on non-differentiated workflows, open infrastructure for IP-sensitive or highly customized processes.

**The $1.5B valuation question:** Is this pricing sustainable distribution and enterprise wrapper around open-source core, or is market pricing future moat creation? Watch Q1 2027 revenue multiples.

### **The Always-On Agent Paradigm**

OpenAI Dots and similar "persistent agent" products (Google's Gemini workplace agents, Microsoft Work IQ) establish new expectation: **agents that work continuously, not just during active sessions**.

**What this enables:**
- Long-running research synthesis
- Continuous monitoring and alerting
- Proactive task preparation
- Multi-day project execution

**What this breaks:**
- Session-based security models
- Per-query cost optimization
- Manual oversight at every step
- Traditional audit trails (too verbose)

**The economic shift:** From "pay per inference" to "pay for continuous capability." OpenAI positioning Dots for Pro and Business Premium (subscription tiers) rather than usage-based API pricing signals recognition that always-on agents require different business models.

**Adoption blocker:** Most enterprises lack **governance for persistent autonomy**. Current controls designed for human-initiated, time-bounded actions. Always-on agents require different risk frameworks: What happens when no human is watching? Who's liable for actions taken at 3 AM? How do you audit 24/7 decision streams?

**The unlock:** Organizations that solve persistent-agent governance first will capture disproportionate value. This is where Microsoft's Work IQ "critical security layer" guidance matters—the architecture must encode constraints agents can't bypass, not rely on agents respecting boundaries.
