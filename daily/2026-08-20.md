## 💡 Insights & Analysis

### The Agentic AI Safety Paradox
As Dawn Song emphasized at Berkeley's summit, agentic AI introduces fundamentally new security challenges because these systems **take actions** rather than just generating information. Traditional AI safety focused on harmful content generation; agentic AI safety must address:
- Operational side effects from tool execution
- Prompt injection attacks that manipulate agent behavior
- Error accumulation across long decision sequences
- Misalignment between agent objectives and user intentions

The Aegis runtime governance framework (arXiv:2608.16891) represents a key architectural pattern: treating model outputs as *proposals* requiring trusted authorization rather than direct execution commands.

### Enterprise AI Deployment Reality Check
The 95% failure rate tells a story about organizational capability, not technology maturity. Successful deployments share common patterns:
1. **Integration over innovation** - Embedding AI into existing workflows rather than launching standalone products
2. **Data work dominates** - 80% of budget on data preparation vs. 20% on models/infrastructure (inverted from typical allocations)
3. **Explicit ROI frameworks** - Measurable outcomes and kill criteria from day one
4. **Private/hybrid patterns emerging** - Sensitive workloads moving to private clusters for compliance; elasticity-demanding workloads staying in cloud

The shift from training to inference as the dominant compute workload means optimization must focus on **serving expertise** (orchestration, observability, compliance) rather than training-cluster management.

### The Multi-Agent Advantage
The Reconstruction benchmark's 2.4x improvement from multi-agent Swiss tournament pipelines validates a key insight: **AI-assisted discovery architecture should mirror scientific peer review**—distributed adversarial validation rather than single-oracle systems. This pattern appears across:
- Google DeepMind's AI Co-Scientist hypothesis tournaments
- The 643-paper Reconstruction benchmark across six domains
- Enterprise deployment patterns favoring agent teams over monolithic systems

### Model Context Protocol (MCP) as Strategic Infrastructure
Salesforce's Headless Data 360 MCP expansion represents a strategic bet: give away the interface layer to own the data/governance layer beneath it. By exposing 200 APIs through MCP, Salesforce enables agents in Claude, ChatGPT, and Cursor to access governed customer context—making Salesforce data infrastructure indispensable even as UI surfaces become commoditized.

This pattern may define the next phase of enterprise software: **infrastructure companies that provide governed data access to any agent** rather than walled-garden applications.
