### 5. **Major US Banks Shift AI Agent Liability to Customers - Citi, PNC, Citizens Update Terms**

**Citibank, PNC Bank, and Citizens Bank** updated their terms of service on **November 18, 2026**, making **customers responsible for any actions third-party AI agents take** in their accounts—including losses from hacking or unauthorized transactions. The updates follow similar policy changes from **JPMorgan and Bank of America** announced last month.

**Key policy:** Customers who grant external AI agents access to accounts (via credentials or API authorization) assume full liability for what those agents do. Banks explicitly state this includes unauthorized access or hacking occurring through the agent. However, **customers can use the banks' own AI agents without additional liability**.

A Citi spokesperson told TechCrunch: "We recently updated our Online User Agreement to provide greater clarity about those risks and to reinforce that customers who choose to grant account access to third-party AI agents are responsible for activities conducted through those agents."

**What this means:** Five major US banks representing approximately **$9.6 trillion in consolidated assets** (JPMorgan $3.4T, Bank of America $2.65T, Citi $1.98T, PNC $610B, Citizens $233B) have now adopted this policy. The coordinated shift signals banks view third-party agent integration as a liability risk rather than innovation opportunity—and positions their own AI offerings as the "safe" alternative. Markets briefly unsettled by prospect that AI tools could help customers avoid unnecessary fees or low-interest deposit accounts.

### 6. **Perplexity Brain Now Creates and Updates Reusable Agent Skills from Experience**

Perplexity announced on **November 18, 2026**, that **Brain**—the personalization system for its Computer agent—can now **create and update reusable agent skills** learned from past work. Brain has written **more than 80,000 personal skills** for over 2 million users, automating recurring procedures without requiring users to re-explain workflows in every session.

**How it works:** Brain implements **work-centered memory** that retains what the agent did, what produced good results, what failed, and what corrections the user made. When the agent encounters similar tasks, it applies learned skills automatically. Perplexity reports Brain increases answer correctness by **25% on tasks Computer has seen before** while reducing costs by **13%**.

**Architecture:** Brain builds a context graph and LLM wiki from session history, then generates executable skills stored in Perplexity's infrastructure. Users get transparency into what's stored but not ownership—data remains on Perplexity's servers. The system supports Agent API Skills, enabling developers to integrate skill-learning capabilities.

**Competitive positioning:** This release comes as Google makes Gemini Tasks free (reaching hundreds of millions) and OpenAI deploys healthcare agents at CVS/Cigna. Perplexity differentiates through persistent, self-improving memory rather than one-shot task execution. Users requiring full data sovereignty are better served by local alternatives (Hermes, OpenClaw with Mem0/Honcho/Obsidian Mind plugins).

## 🛠️ Tools & Frameworks

### sand3d: LLM-Native 3D Modeling in Python (2,000+ GitHub Stars)

**sand3d**, the Apache-2.0 Python library for writing 3D objects as short, LLM-friendly programs, continues gaining traction with **2,000+ GitHub stars** following its November 5 release by French AI startup Sandflow. The library represents 3D objects as concise Python code that's **2-6× more token-efficient** than Blender Python, CadQuery, or FeatureScript equivalents.

**Key features:**
- **Short:** Parts take 2-6× fewer tokens than alternatives
- **Self-documenting:** Named parts, edges, faces, and joints
- **Runs on Blender:** Full compatibility with Blender's ecosystem
- **106,094 objects:** Largest 3D-as-code dataset, each as a short Python program

**Why it matters:** LLMs traditionally struggle with 3D generation—they hallucinate vertex coordinates, write Blender Python that silently fails, or produce code so verbose it consumes half a context window. sand3d solves this by providing a domain-specific language that's both executable and naturally readable to frontier models.

**Example use case:** An agent generating a mechanical part might previously require 1,200 tokens of Blender Python; sand3d reduces this to 200-400 tokens with clear structure. The library enables agents to build, modify, and reason about 3D geometry without coordinate-level manipulation.

**Dataset:** The Sand3D dataset on Hugging Face provides 106,094 pre-built objects with renders, offering training data for 3D-capable agents or direct reference for code generation.

## 💡 Insights & Analysis

### 1. **Public Markets Meet Frontier AI: The Anthropic Watershed**

Anthropic's $83/share pricing (above range) and today's trading debut marks the moment frontier AI labs face public market discipline. Unlike private funding rounds where safety positioning could be purely narrative, **quarterly earnings will force disclosure** on:
- Safety incident rates and response protocols
- Model development costs vs. API revenue
- Customer concentration risk (if few enterprises dominate usage)
- Competitive positioning vs. Microsoft/OpenAI/Google efficiency plays

The **6× oversubscription** signals institutional investors believe AI infrastructure is sufficiently mature to support public company fundamentals. But the test comes in quarters 2-4 of 2027: can Anthropic's 55% API share and premium safety positioning justify costs when Microsoft delivers 98% quality at 40% price?

**Governance implications:** The Class F share structure (50.1% founder voting control) will face scrutiny if incidents occur. Public shareholders have economic exposure but limited control—a model other AI labs may adopt for IPOs, but one that concentrates accountability in a small group during a period of rapid capability expansion.

### 2. **The Price-Performance Compression Accelerates**

This week's model releases (GPT-6.2 Astra 20% cheaper, Gemini 4 Neon at $3/M tokens) show **pricing compression happening faster than capability divergence**. When OpenAI reduces Astra by 20% and Sol delivers within 0.5 points at 1/5 cost, the message is clear: **frontier quality is commoditizing**.

**The new battleground:** Not "who has the smartest model?" but "who delivers acceptable quality at sustainable economics for 24/7 agent operations?" Microsoft's aggressive positioning (98% quality at 40% cost) forces OpenAI and Google to compete on efficiency while maintaining premium positioning.

**Agent economics matter:** With 80% of API tokens from agents, reducing per-token cost by 20-80% makes previously uneconomical continuous operation viable. A customer service agent running 24/7 at $0.08/1K input tokens ($80/M) vs. $0.24/1K ($240/M) is the difference between ROI-positive and budget-breaking at enterprise scale.

### 3. **Banks Building Moats Through Liability Shifting**

The coordinated November 18 terms updates from Citi, PNC, and Citizens (following JPMorgan and BofA in October) isn't about risk management—it's about **competitive moat construction**. By making third-party agent liability the customer's problem while exempting their own agents, **five banks controlling $9.6T in assets** are steering customers toward proprietary AI offerings.

**What this blocks:**
- Personal finance agents that optimize across multiple banks
- AI-powered switching services that find better rates/terms
- Automated fee avoidance and account optimization
- Cross-bank financial planning and liquidity management

**The deeper game:** If third-party agents become primary interface for financial decisions, banks lose control of the customer relationship and product discovery. By creating liability friction, banks ensure their own agents (which they control, monetize, and optimize for bank profit) become the "safe" default.

**Regulatory watch:** Consumer protection agencies may scrutinize whether this practice constitutes anticompetitive behavior or unfair limitation of customer rights. The question: can banks unilaterally block innovation in customer financial management through terms-of-service updates?

### 4. **Memory and Personalization: The Next Agent Battleground**

Perplexity's Brain announcement (80K+ skills generated) reveals the **emerging competitive dimension beyond raw model capability**: **persistent, self-improving memory that transfers across sessions**. When Brain delivers 25% better correctness and 13% lower cost on repeated tasks, it demonstrates that **agents that learn compound advantage over one-shot executors**.

**The stack emerging:**
1. **Foundation capability:** GPT-6.2, Gemini 4 Neon, Claude Opus 5.5 (increasingly commoditized)
2. **Orchestration:** LangChain, Microsoft Copilot, etc. (connecting agents to tools)
3. **Memory layer:** Perplexity Brain, Mem0, Honcho, Obsidian Mind (learning from experience)
4. **Skills library:** Reusable procedures refined over time

**Why memory matters:** A customer service agent that remembers how a specific company handles returns, billing disputes, and account inquiries becomes more valuable over months of deployment. Without memory, every interaction starts from zero—with memory, the agent improves while costs decrease.

**Data sovereignty tension:** Perplexity's Brain runs on their infrastructure (users don't own the memory). Local alternatives (OpenClaw, Hermes with plugins) provide data sovereignty at the cost of convenience. Enterprise customers in regulated industries will increasingly demand on-premises memory systems.

### 5. **The "Free Agent" Experiment: Google's Strategic Gambit**

Google's November 17 decision to **make Tasks in Gemini free** in six countries (reaching "hundreds of millions") is the most aggressive consumer agent play yet. By removing the paywall entirely (previously AI Pro required), Google is betting that **mainstream agent adoption requires zero-friction entry**.

**The calculus:**
- **Cost:** Gemini 4 Hydrogen inference at scale (subsidized)
- **Benefit:** User behavior data, engagement lock-in, defaults for commerce/bookings
- **Risk:** Unmonetized usage if adoption doesn't convert to paid tiers or commerce

**Why now:** OpenAI has ChatGPT Pro at $200/month with agents. Perplexity has Computer behind paywalls. Microsoft has Copilot integrated with Office 365. Google's consumer AI strategy (outside Workspace) needed differentiation—free agentic capabilities for hundreds of millions is that wedge.

**What to watch:** Conversion rates from free Tasks to paid tiers, revenue from Google Pay transactions facilitated by agents, and whether "free but limited" becomes the norm (forcing OpenAI/Perplexity to adjust pricing).

### 6. **Coordination Signals in Bank Policy Updates**

The **simultaneous November 18 updates** from Citi, PNC, and Citizens (following JPMorgan/BofA in October) raise questions about coordination. Did these institutions independently arrive at identical language around third-party agent liability within weeks of each other, or was there industry coordination?

**Possible explanations:**
1. **Industry trade groups:** Banking associations may have circulated model language
2. **Legal counsel overlap:** Major law firms advising multiple banks on AI risk
3. **Regulatory guidance:** Informal signals from OCC, Fed, or FDIC on agent liability
4. **Competitive response:** Each bank monitoring competitors and matching policy

**Regulatory implications:** If coordination occurred through trade groups, regulators may scrutinize whether this constitutes anticompetitive behavior. The timing (within 4 weeks across 5 major banks) suggests more than coincidence.

**Consumer impact:** With $9.6T in assets now behind these terms, the practical effect is to make third-party agent integration high-risk for consumers. This may slow AI-driven financial innovation or push it underground (screen scraping, credential sharing without formal authorization).
