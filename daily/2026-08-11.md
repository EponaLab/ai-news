### Agent Orchestration Advances Mathematical Research Without Breakthrough Insight

Claude's improvement of Riemann zeta hypothesis bound from 41.6% to 67.2% demonstrates that **multi-agent orchestration can contribute to hard mathematical problems through synthesis and validation** rather than requiring novel insight:

**What the system did:**
- Synthesized recent papers (not original proof attempts)
- Orchestrated 60 subagents for parallel validation
- Executed 2,400 shell commands and thousands of numerical checks
- Consumed 31M tokens across two sessions

**What the system did NOT do:**
- Prove the Riemann hypothesis
- Generate fundamentally new mathematical techniques
- Produce insights requiring mathematical creativity

**Implications:**
1. **Agent strength is in systematic exploration, not creativity** — valuable for validation and incremental improvement
2. **Orchestration cost** (31M tokens) must be weighed against value of marginal improvement
3. **Suitable problems:** Those benefiting from exhaustive search, numerical validation, and synthesis of existing literature
4. **Unsuitable problems:** Those requiring conceptual breakthroughs or novel proof techniques

**Research ethics concern:** Mathematicians accusing OpenAI's Astra proofs of plagiarism highlights tension between "synthesis" and "reproduction without attribution."
